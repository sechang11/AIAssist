What the measurement actually showed

Fifty text messages chosen to break things, scored two ways, three passes each. Mechanically, by rules that check structure and whether every date, time, number and name survived. And semantically, by a model reading each rewrite against the original and asking only whether the commitment, the facts and the roles are intact. The two disagree, and the disagreement is the finding.

Faithfulness, by model size

Does the rewrite still say what the writer said? This ordering is strictly by parameter count, which the mechanical score did not show.

Where the two measures disagree

Fine-tuning raised the mechanical score and lowered faithfulness, at both sizes. It taught the form of a good answer at the cost of the substance. Bars are means over three passes; the small models swing by up to sixteen points between identical runs, which is why single runs were never a ranking.

What the mechanical score could not see

Original
sorry i cant make saturday, ive got my sisters wedding. have a great time though
Rewrite, scored perfectly clean
i hope you have a fantastic time at your sister's wedding! i'll be thinking of you

The wedding is why the writer cannot come. This hands it to the reader. Every date and name survived, the three alternatives differed from each other, none repeated a neighbour, so nothing complained.

Original
yes if we can finish by 4, otherwise ill have to skip it
Rewrite, scored perfectly clean
i'd love to join but unfortunately can't make it this time due to other commitments

A conditional yes became a flat no, with an invented reason. The "4" survived in another beat, so the fact check was satisfied.

What it means

Reshaping the task was still the biggest single win. Asking one call to segment, paraphrase, keep cells independent and preserve every fact is a bookkeeping problem, and small models never struggled with the writing, only the bookkeeping. Moving it into code took a 1.5B from 10% to 58% mechanically and from 51% to 70% on faithfulness. Both measures agree about that.

But the ceiling I reported earlier was the instrument, not the task. The mechanical score plateaus around 70% because it runs out of ability to tell the models apart, not because they stop improving. Faithfulness keeps climbing with size all the way to the 14B.

Fine-tuning optimised the metric and damaged the product. The fine-tuned 0.5B is the best small model on the mechanical score and the second worst on faithfulness. It learned to produce well-formed, distinct, structurally clean alternatives, and got meaning wrong more often while doing it. Untrained, the same model simply refused more: a third of its output was the writer's own words handed back.

The 7B and 14B rows are single passes. Everything else is a mean over three, with the grey band on each bar showing the observed range. Those two were too slow to repeat in the time available, so treat them as indicative rather than measured.

One caveat that matters. The judge is a 14B scoring output that includes its own, so its position at the top may be self-preference. The ordering across the three models it did not produce holds regardless, but the absolute numbers deserve a different judge before anyone leans on them.

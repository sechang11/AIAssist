What the models can actually do

Fifty text messages chosen to break things, scored mechanically. "Clean" means no problem of any kind on that message: the shape is right, every date, time, number and name survived into all three tone columns, nothing was invented, the alternatives genuinely differ, and neighbouring beats do not repeat.

Clean score by model

Two changes moved this. Restructuring the task from one call that builds the whole grid into one short call per beat, and then fine-tuning the smallest model on four thousand examples from a larger one.

What training actually changed

The 0.5B already kept every hard fact, because the pipeline discards a rewrite that loses one. Its failure was distinctness: three alternatives barely different from each other, which is safe and useless. That is a learned behaviour, and it is what moved.

What it means

Model size stopped buying quality well below 14B. Off the shelf, the 1.5B and the 14B both land on 74%, and the 14B takes thirty times as long per message. Whatever the last quarter is, it is not a capability problem, so there is nothing to be gained by shopping for a bigger model.

The task shape mattered five times more than the model. Asking one call to segment a message, paraphrase every beat, keep each cell independent and preserve every fact is a bookkeeping problem. Small models never struggled with the writing: output stayed fluent and correctly formed while the content went wrong. Moving the bookkeeping into code took the 1.5B from 14% to 74%.

Training closed most of what remained at the smallest size. The teacher was a 7B running on the same machine, the data was four thousand eight hundred filtered examples, and the run took under ten minutes. No paid API touched any of it.

Hard facts sit at 100% everywhere. That is not the models being careful. A rewrite that loses a day, a time, a number or a name is discarded and that beat falls back to the writer's own words. Losing the retoning on one beat is a far smaller harm than losing the time they agreed to meet.
